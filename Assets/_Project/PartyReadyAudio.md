# Party arrival gate and movement audio

Night transitions now use two readiness gates. The existing gate covers every peer with black before loading. The arrival gate then holds that black screen until every connected peer has all roster player objects and their movement/oxygen components spawned, the night clock spawned, and its own living player camera/listener ready. Dead peers wait for a functioning spectator camera. The host sends the expected roster with the transition token and scene; acknowledgements from old transitions or other scenes cannot release it. Disconnects update the remaining roster. A slow client remains connected under black while loading.

Once everyone acknowledges readiness, the host schedules the night card and fade on a shared server-clock timestamp. Night time, gameplay and player movement audio remain paused under the curtain. The loading camera stays active throughout the handoff. Offline Play keeps a local readiness check.

`PlayerSurfaceAudio` is attached automatically by `PlayerController.Start`, including remote replicated players. It uses the existing owner-written movement/jump snapshot and observed displacement; no extra NetworkBehaviour, per-step RPC, or network prefab modification is required. Steps need actual grounded travel and movement input. Sprinting has faster/louder cadence. Airborne travel, blocked movement, menus, death, transitions and teleports do not create step bursts. Jump and landing cues follow the replicated takeoff sequence and actual landing timestamp; a joining observer initializes current history instead of replaying old jumps.

The 12 original synthesized mono WAVs under `Assets/Resources/PlayerMovement` provide four step variations, takeoff and landing for each surface. The bunker uses a resonant boot impact and a spatial echo filter (110 ms delay, 0.3 decay, 0.23 wet mix); exterior movement uses dry granular sand/grit sounds. The physical bunker trigger chooses the bank at the sound origin. Separate 3D sources preserve a short bunker echo tail when exiting. Both sources use 1.5 m minimum and 22 m maximum range. These presentation sounds do not change monster hearing/difficulty.

`PartyReadyAudioChecks.Build` validates every sound asset, the network scene identity guard, and the production build. Development-only `-partyReadyAudioTest host` / `client` exercises the actual menu and Night 3/Night 4 scenes over Unity Transport: a five-second client-camera delay, clock/black hold, dead-peer spectator arrival, bunker and sand walk/run/takeoff/landing sounds on the owner and observing peer, echo setup, idle silence, walls and teleports. All peers need the updated build because the curtain message protocol now includes the arrival gate and scheduled reveal timestamp.

Validation: production Windows build succeeded; all 12 mono clips loaded and canonical scene IDs passed. A graphics-enabled host/client production run passed 39 assertions. Neither peer log contained managed exceptions, network synchronization/spawn errors, shader failures or error logs.
